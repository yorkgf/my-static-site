AP Business with Personal Finance Syllabus

AP Business with Personal Finance is an introductory, college-level business and personal finance course. Students explore the business disciplines of entrepreneurship, marketing, finance, accounting, and management through real-world business application, case studies, and project-based learning. In addition, students learn and apply all the National Standards for Personal Financial Education created by the Council for Economic Education and the Jump$tart Coalition for Personal Financial Literacy.

This course is designed to be the equivalent of a one-semester college introduction to business course and a one-semester college personal finance course.

Signature Projects

  • Business Canvas Project (Units 1–4) — Students build a complete Business Model Canvas for their own business idea, including market research, financial projections, and a SWOT/Porter's Five Forces analysis.
  • Financial Advisor Project (Unit 5) — Students prepare and communicate financial advice for a fictional household covering budgeting, education funding, home-buying, and retirement.

Upon completion of this course, students will be able to:

  1. Understand business fundamentals — Explain how businesses create value, operate, compete, and make ethical decisions.
  2. Apply marketing concepts — Analyze how businesses use market research, consumer behavior, and marketing strategies.
  3. Master personal finance — Apply concepts of saving, borrowing, credit, debt, and investing.
  4. Analyze business finance — Interpret financial statements and understand business accounting.
  5. Evaluate management strategies — Use strategic frameworks like SWOT and Porter's Five Forces.
  6. Develop professional skills — Demonstrate communication, collaboration, and decision-making skills.

Case Study Videos

Unit 1 — Business Foundations

1.1 Bombas — “Better Socks, Bigger Mission”

“Buy one, donate one” athletic socks. Gap, social enterprise, and risk–return (deal with Daymond John).

1.1 VANCL 凡客 — “人民快时尚”

Affordable apparel e-commerce that grew huge, then collapsed — a cautionary case.

1.1 ofo 小黄车 — bike-share collapse

Dockless bikes everywhere, but upkeep cost more than revenue; burned through billions and collapsed in 2018–19.

1.2 Cola vs Chinese Soda — cost advantage

Why can Coca-Cola profit at 3 yuan a bottle while domestic sodas lose money at 7?

1.2 得物 Dewu — differentiation through trust

The same sneakers sell elsewhere for less, yet Dewu charges more — authentication as the edge.

1.2 海底捞 Haidilao — service advantage

The same hot pot, but legendary service that commands premium pricing.

1.2 元气森林 Genki Forest — marketing positioning

“0 sugar” positioning, KOL blitz, and 日系 packaging built a $15B brand.

1.2 钟薛高 Zhong Xue Gao — hype without substance

Premium ice cream (20–60 yuan) via branding and social-media hype; no real differentiator, bankrupt 2024.

1.3 新东方 / 东方甄选 — PESTEL pivot

Double-reduction policy forced a pivot to livestream commerce — a legal/environmental factor reshaping a business.

1.3 Pop Mart / Labubu — social trend

A toy whose viral social trend (collecting, sharing, status) created a Southeast Asian resale market.

1.3 Shein — global fast-fashion giant

Ultra-cheap fashion across 150+ countries — one of the world’s most downloaded and controversial shopping apps.

1.4 Pop Mart — how blind boxes began

From a small blind-box shop to a multi-billion-dollar company — how a business idea originates.

1.4 Copa Di Vino — rejected on Shark Tank

Single-serve premium wine; rejected by every shark twice, yet built a $60M+ business.

1.5 Patagonia — values-driven company

A company that actually lives its environmental values. Mission-Driven Company (related ▶)

1.5 Seesaw Coffee — abandoning vision

What remains of a specialty-coffee brand after it chases a price war?

1.6 携程 Ctrip — business ethics

When a business has the data to charge loyal customers more — should it? (Big-data price discrimination.)

1.8 Supply Chain — a century of evolution

小Lin说 case study on how modern supply chains developed (3.6M views).

1.8 Zara — fast-fashion supply chain

How Zara’s vertically integrated supply chain powers its speed-to-market.

Unit 2 — Marketing

2.4 Dyson — from vacuum revolution to global brand

Product innovation story illustrating the product life cycle and product-led growth.

2.5 Penetration Pricing Strategy

An explainer on how and why firms set low initial prices to gain market share.

2.5 Costco — the $40M loss-leader chicken

Costco loses $40 million a year on its rotisserie chicken — completely intentionally.
