Two Money Terms

Revenue & Profit

Revenue

Selling income, before expenses

Profit

Left after all expenses

Profit = Revenue − Total Cost
Applying It

Revenue & Profit — two models

  • 凡客: low price × high volume — until over-expansion broke the model
  • Bombas: premium price × fewer units (makes 2 pairs per sale — one sold, one donated)
  • Candy seller: revenue = everything collected; profit = left after restocking

Core Concept

"You can't serve everyone"

Severity? How many share it? Can we solve it better? Will they pay? Can we reach them?
Warning"Serve everyone" = wrong answer
Applying It

Won by selecting

  • Bombas → premium, mission-minded buyers
  • Candy seller → only his own grade's hallway

Lost by serving everyone

  • 凡客 — shirts → everything; inventory blew up → collapsed
Core Concept

Value

Definition
Worth to customers = highest price they'd pay
RelationshipValue rises with problem-solution fit
Applying It

Value — in the examples

  • Bombas: buyers pay ~$12 (comfort + mission = high value)
  • 凡客: price-sensitive buyers → value via affordability
  • Candy seller: same bar worth more during 3rd period than at a supermarket
The Topic's Core Pair

Value creation vs. value capture

Value creationValue capture
Is aboutthe customerthe business
Meansproduct responds to a problem/need/wantprice higher than cost
Flowsbenefit to customerprofit to business
OrderCreate first → then capture
Applying It

Creation vs. capture — in the examples

  • Bombas: creates (comfort + mission) → captures (premium price)
  • 凡客: created (affordable fashion) + captured — until over-expansion eroded both
  • Candy seller: creates (snack at moment of want) → captures ($1.50 − $0.50 = $1.00/bar)
  • MoviePass: created value but captured none → shut down
Watch the trapMost common exam trap = putting capture definition inside a creation question.
Worked Example

The candy numbers

20-bar box = $10 → cost = $0.50/bar. Sells each for $1.50.

Value captured = $1.50 − $0.50 = $1.00 / bar

Customer side

Would have paid $2, pays $1.50 → keeps $0.50.

Why the trade happens

Both sides better off → voluntary.

Cautionary Tale

MoviePass — create without capture

  • $10/month for a movie every day
  • MoviePass paid theaters near full price
  • A member watching 4 movies/month cost several × $10
  • Price − cost = negative → shut down 2019
Value creation ✓ Value capture ✗
Cautionary Tale

ofo — same failure, different country

ofo 小黄车 — bikes on every corner, dirt cheap:

  • Users loved it
  • But wear + upkeep cost more than revenue → burned through billions
  • Collapsed 2018–19
WarningPopularity + price below cost = creation WITHOUT capture.
Core Concept

Value proposition

Definition
How product solves a problem + why choose it over alternatives
For [target] who has [problem], our product delivers [benefit]. Unlike [alternative]…
Applying It

Value proposition — in the examples

Bombas

"For people who want comfortable socks — and for every pair you buy, we donate one."

Candy seller

"For students stuck on campus who want a snack now — $1.50 in the hallway, unlike an empty vending machine."

RecipeOne sentence: customer + problem + solution + edge.

Check Yourself

Practice — 6 questions

3 on one stimulus (LumiDesk) 3 standalone
Read → pause → commit to an answer → then reveal.
Stimulus

LumiDesk

Scenario

LumiDesk makes height-adjustable study desks for students in small apartments.

  • Cost to manufacture: $90
  • Customers value it at: $260
  • Sells for: $180
  • Tutoring centers buy desks → students use them in lessons
C = $90 value = $260 P = $180
Q1 Who is the consumer?

For desks bought by tutoring centers, who is the consumer?

Athe tutoring centers
BLumiDesk
Cthe students who use the desks
Dthe parents who pay tuition
Answer C

Why

Centers bought (customer); students use (consumers).

Traps: A = customer in a consumer question. D = different transaction (parents → tuition).
Q2 Compute value captured

What is the value captured by LumiDesk per desk?

A$80
B$90
C$170
D$260
Answer B ($90)
Value captured = $180 − $90 = $90
Trap: A ($80) = $260 − $180 (customer's benefit). Same three numbers, different formula.
Q3 Why is it value creation?

Which best explains why LumiDesk demonstrates value creation?

Asold at a price higher than manufacturing cost
Bresponds to the space problem faced by students in small apartments
Cgenerates revenue from individuals and tutoring centers
Dmanufacturing cost is low relative to competitors
Answer B

Why

Value creation = responds to a customer's problem/need/want.

Trap: A = capture definition in a creation question — the most common trap.
Q4 Problem-solution fit

A snack company sees gym members want high-protein food after workouts, and puts refrigerated protein snacks in gym vending machines. Which concept?

Avalue capture
Bbarrier to entry
Cproblem-solution fit
Da need converted into a want
Answer C

Why

A want was identified, a matching product built, placed at the point the want occurs.

Trap: B — real Topic 1.2 term used too early. Recognizing a word isn't enough — it must match.
Q5 Goods vs. services

HomeGuard: one-time price for a camera + doorbell plus monthly fee for 24-hour monitoring. Which describes its products?

Agoods only
Bservices only
Cboth goods and services
Dneither
Answer C

Why

Camera = good; monitoring = service.

Trap: D mixes needs/wants (why buy) with goods/services (what's sold).
Q6 Choosing customers

A new meal-delivery startup with limited funding. Which best reflects how businesses address market opportunities?

Aserve every possible customer in the city
Bselect a specific group whose problem you can solve better
Ctarget customers with the mildest problems
Ddelay choosing until the product is perfected
Answer B

Why

A business cannot satisfy all customers → it selects.

Trap: A — "serve everyone" is the wrong answer.
