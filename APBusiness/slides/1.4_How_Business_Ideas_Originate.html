Practice — 6 questions

Read → pause → commit to an answer → then reveal.
3 on one stimulus (ParkClean)· 3 standalone
Stimulus

Scenario

Marcus Chen lives near a large public park. He notices:

1. The park has no trash cans near the picnic areas — visitors carry trash for 15 minutes before finding a bin, and many leave garbage on the ground 2. A new city ordinance just passed requiring parks to be kept clean or the city will cut the parks budget 3. A classmate already runs a small lawn-mowing service in the neighborhood and is earning steady money

Marcus is thinking about starting a business to clean the park on weekends.

Practice Question
Q1Classify the source

The fact that the park has no trash cans near the picnic area — and visitors leave garbage on the ground — is best described as what type of business idea source?

AAn opportunity created by new technology
BA gap — an unmet need that no one is currently serving
CAn existing business that Marcus is copying
DA risk factor that discourages new businesses

Pause — is this something new happening, or a need nobody is meeting?

Answer B

Why

A gap is a need or want that is not currently being met. The park needs cleaning, nobody is providing that service, and visitors are leaving garbage behind — that is an unmet need.

A — no new technology in the scenario. A gap is about observation (watching a problem nobody is solving), not about innovation.
C — the classmate's lawn-mowing service is a separate business. Marcus isn't copying it; he's seeing a different unmet need.
Practice Question
Q2Viability check

Which factor is most relevant to whether Marcus's park-cleaning idea is viable?

AWhether Marcus enjoys being outdoors
BWhether someone (the city or park visitors) is willing and able to pay for the service
CWhether the park has flowers or trees
DWhether Marcus's classmates think the idea is cool
Answer B

Why

Viability depends on whether the business can survive — and that means there must be willing and able buyers. If no one will pay, the idea isn't viable no matter how good it sounds.

A — enjoyment is a personal motivation, not a viability factor. A business can be viable even if the owner doesn't enjoy it.
Key PointViability is about the market, not about the person.
Practice Question
Q3Risk–return judgment

Marcus would need to spend $200 on cleaning supplies and give up his Saturday mornings. The city might pay him $80 per weekend. Which statement best describes the risk–return tradeoff?

AThe return is guaranteed because the city passed the ordinance
BThe risk is low (small investment) and the return is modest — a low-risk, low-return opportunity
CThis is a high-risk, high-return opportunity because the city might change the rules
DThere is no risk because Marcus is providing a service, not selling a product
Answer B

Why

$200 is a small investment, $80/weekend is a modest return, and the ordinance supports demand. This is a classic low-risk, low-return scenario — small stakes, small but steady gain.

A — "guaranteed" is an absolute. The city could cut the budget, change priorities, or find a cheaper contractor. No return is ever guaranteed.
C — the investment is $200 and the scenario gives no reason to expect massive earnings. High-risk, high-return requires large stakes or large potential gains — neither applies here.
Practice Question
Q4Combining sources

A school cafeteria currently serves only hot food. Students have asked for fresh salads and cold sandwiches, but the cafeteria hasn't added them. A student starts selling homemade salads from a cooler outside the cafeteria entrance.

This business idea combines which source(s)?

AOpportunity only — the student is innovating
BExisting business only — the student is copying the cafeteria
CA gap (unmet demand for cold food) combined with the existing market (the cafeteria already serves food)
DNeither — selling food at school is not a real business
Answer C

Why

There is a gap — students want cold food and nobody is selling it. There is also an existing market — the cafeteria already operates in the same space. The student's idea comes from both sources at once.

Exam NoteMost real ideas combine more than one source. The exam rewards you for recognizing all the sources present, not just the loudest one.
Practice Question
Q5Matching strengths to opportunity

Two students both notice the same gap: the school has no tutoring service for math. Student A is excellent at math and has free time after school. Student B struggles in math but has a lot of savings from a part-time job.

Which statement is most accurate?

AStudent B is better positioned because money matters more than skill
BStudent A is better positioned because their strengths match the opportunity
CBoth are equally positioned because they both identified the same gap
DNeither can succeed because the school doesn't officially allow tutoring
Answer B

Why

Identifying a gap is only the first step. Student A has the skill (math ability) and the availability (free time) — their strengths match the opportunity. Student B has money but lacks the core skill.

Key PointA good idea in the right hands becomes a viable business. A good idea in the wrong hands — with the wrong skills or resources — fails.
Practice Question
Q6Risk and return are connected

A student wants to start a business. They consider two options:

  • Option A: Buy candy for $50 and sell it at school (small investment, proven demand)
  • Option B: Spend $2,000 developing a homework app (large investment, unproven demand)

Which statement about the risk–return tradeoff is most accurate?

AOption B is better because higher investment always means higher profit
BOption A has lower risk and lower potential return; Option B has higher risk and higher potential return
CBoth options have the same level of risk because both are student businesses
DOption A is riskier because candy is a physical product that can melt
Answer B

Why

This is the risk–return tradeoff in its simplest form. Option A is low-risk (small investment, known demand) with low return (small profit). Option B is high-risk (big investment, no proven buyers) with high potential return (if the app succeeds, it could scale to thousands of students).

A — "higher investment always means higher profit" uses an absolute. Higher investment means higher potential return — but also higher potential loss.
Exam Note"Always" and "guaranteed" are trap words — they show up in almost every risk–return question.
