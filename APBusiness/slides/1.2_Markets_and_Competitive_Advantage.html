Lesson

Voluntary exchange

Inside every market: voluntary exchange — both sides walk away better off.

From 1.1: student paid $1.50 for a bar he valued at $2 → kept $0.50. Seller captured $1.

Key PointRevenue for sellers + value for buyers — that's why trade happens voluntarily.
Lesson

Two forces push against each other

Sellers

Seek higher prices → profit

Buyers

Attempt lower prices → savings

Key PointThe balance determines the market price.
Lesson

Market price — the mechanism

A competitive market settles at a prevailing market price — no individual seller can hold above it.

DayWhat happens
MondayNew kid opens at $1.25; buyers move to him
TuesdayOur seller matches; new kid drops to $1.00
WednesdayBoth sit at $1.00 — neither can go back up
Exam signalA business raises price above going rate → loses sales to rivals = testing this concept.

How competitive is a market?

Three factors (from the exam creators):

  1. Number of rival businesses
  2. Degree of product differentiation
  3. Extent rivals can offer similar products at a lower price
More rivals + less differentiation + easy undercutting = more competitive

Lesson

Identical-product markets → efficiency

Markets where products are identical — buyers choose purely on price.

Key PointIn identical-product markets, efficiency IS the strategy.

Candy bar: identical bars → the winner is whoever restocks cheaper.

Lesson

Cola — efficiency at scale

Why does Coca-Cola win at 3 yuan while some brands lose money at 7 yuan?

Scale: billions of bottles Supply chain: global bottling Per-unit cost drops
Key PointNo one pays a premium for a commodity. Cheapest efficient producer wins.
Lesson

Differentiated markets → superiority

Products with distinguishing features — demonstrate why yours is superior.

Five routes (from exam creators):

1. Quality 2. Unique features 3. Customer service 4. Lower prices 5. Marketing
Lesson

Five routes — real examples

Route 2 — Unique features

Route 3 — Customer service

Route 5 — Marketing

Lesson

⚠️ When marketing alone isn't enough

钟薛高 — ordinary ice cream at 20–60 yuan through 国风 branding + hype.

Product itself had no real distinguishing features. Once consumers saw through it → bankrupt 2024.

Key PointReal differentiation lives in the product, not just the marketing.
Lesson

The escape from the price war

Our seller adds cold drinks out of an insulated bag — no identical rival for that.

Key PointDifferentiation is how a business exits a pure price contest.

得物 did the same at scale: added authentication layer → price competition → feature competition.

Lesson

Barriers to entry

Barriers to entry
Obstacles that make it difficult for new firms to compete.
BarrierExample
Intellectual propertyPatent protecting a medicine
RegulationsRules that limit rivals
High startup costsWhy new airlines are rare
Low prices from scaleCoca-Cola at 3 yuan
Lesson

Barriers in the hallway

Upfront cash for bulk box Parent with warehouse card Being first — regular customers
Key PointSmall barriers, same principle: obstacles at the front door.

Businesses work to create and strengthen barriers — every barrier held = competition avoided.

Lesson

Monopoly

Monopoly
A market without competition — only one business, unique product.

That was the candy seller before Monday morning: one seller, unique product, $1.50 freely set.

Key PointA monopoly may protect itself by maintaining barriers to entry (e.g., government license for a utility).
Lesson

Advantage vs. monopoly

Competitive advantageMonopoly
MeansOutperforming rivalsNo rivals at all
Contest?Yes — you're winningNo — contest doesn't exist
Key PointAdvantage = winning a contest. Monopoly = absence of the contest.

Practice — 6 questions

Read → pause → commit to an answer → then reveal.
3 on one stimulus (FreshCut)· 3 standalone
Stimulus

Scenario

Marcus Webb owns FreshCut, one of nine stands selling strawberries at a farmers market.

  • All nine sell nearly identical strawberries
  • Last month Marcus charged $6 while others charged $5 → sales fell → he returned to $5
  • Two options:
  • 1. Invest in equipment to cut costs below rivals
  • 2. Sell prepared strawberry desserts no other stand offers
Practice
Q1Why did sales fall at $6?
ABuyers switched to rival stands selling identical strawberries at market price
BMarcus' strawberries were lower quality
CThe farmers market operates as a monopoly
DRaising prices always reduces total sales
Answer A

Why

Nearly identical products → buyer facing higher price walks to the next stand. Competitive market settles at prevailing price ($5).

D — "always" = fake universal law. A differentiated business can raise prices.
Practice
Q2The dessert option
ASeeking competitive advantage through product differentiation
BStrengthening a barrier to entry
CCharging the prevailing market price
DOperating in a commodity market
Answer A

Why

Dessert no rival sells = distinguishing feature → moves FreshCut out of identical-products contest.

Key distinctionUniqueness a rival can copy tomorrow = differentiation. Obstacle they can't get past = barrier.
Practice
Q3The cost-cutting option
ALower costs let him profitably charge the lowest price for an identical product
BLower costs differentiate his strawberries from rivals
CLower costs make the market less competitive for all stands
DLower costs guarantee increased market share
Answer A

Why

In identical-product markets, businesses seek advantage by producing as efficiently as possible to charge the lowest price.

B — cost lives inside the business; differentiation lives inside the product. Cheaper-to-produce strawberries are still identical to the buyer.
Practice
Q4Reading a data table
AppYear 1Year 3
SwiftRide38%52%
GoCab35%30%
UrbanLift27%18%
Practice
Q4What does the data support?
ASwiftRide's market share grew while both rivals' shares declined
BSwiftRide gained share because it charged the lowest prices
CUrbanLift's total revenue declined between year 1 and year 3
DGoCab served more customers in year 3 than in year 1
Answer A

Why

SwiftRide 38→52, GoCab 35→30, UrbanLift 27→18. A states exactly what the numbers show.

B offers a cause the table never shows. C confuses market share (%) with revenue ($).
Practice
Q5Large-scale low prices

A large beverage company produces at such scale it can profitably charge prices new rivals cannot match. Best describes the effect?

AVoluntary exchange
BA monopoly
CA commodity market
DA barrier to entry
Answer D

Why

Barriers to entry include low prices from large-scale operation. A price entrants can't survive = obstacle at the front door.

B — scale pricing deters new rivals, but the company still competes with existing ones. Monopoly = only business in the market.
Practice
Q6The utility

HydroRegion is the only company licensed to supply electricity in its region. Best describes its position?

AA commodity seller in a highly competitive market
BA competitive advantage achieved through differentiation
CA rival business seeking increased market share
DA monopoly protected by a legal barrier to entry
Answer D

Why

One business, unique service, no competition = monopoly. Government license = the barrier.

B — competitive advantage means outperforming rivals — but HydroRegion has no rivals.
