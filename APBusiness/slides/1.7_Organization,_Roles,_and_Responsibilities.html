Scenario 1

A business has one owner, who is personally liable for all debts, and funds growth through savings and a bank loan.

Key PointWhat is the legal structure?

Run the three questions:

  1. How many owners? → One
  2. Who pays debts? → The owner personally
  3. How funded? → Savings and loans
Answer

Sole proprietorship.

One owner + personal liability + self-funded. The other options fail on the first question:

  • Partnership requires 2+ owners
  • LLC requires a liability shield
  • Corporation requires shareholders and a board
Key PointStart with question 1 and eliminate.
Lesson

Scenario 2

Two friends start a delivery service. They shake hands, split profits equally, and file no legal paperwork. A customer's package is damaged.

Key PointWho is liable for the damage?
AOnly the friend who made the delivery
BOnly the friend who took the order
CThe business alone — neither friend is personally liable
DBoth friends — personally and jointly
Answer

D. Both friends — personally and jointly.

A partnership with no filings = personal liability for all partners. The exam's favorite trap: liability is shared, not divided by who did the work. The friend who didn't make the delivery still pays.

Exam NoteTrap: A and B. Partnership liability doesn't sort by whose hand was on the package. Both partners are liable for all business debts.
Lesson

Scenario 3

A business has three owners who filed legal paperwork. The owners make all decisions together, and the business's debts do not reach their personal savings. The business has no shareholders and no board.

Key PointWhat is the legal structure?
APartnership
BCorporation
CSole proprietorship
DLLC
Answer

D. LLC.

Run the three questions:

  1. How many owners? → Three (eliminates sole proprietorship)
  2. Who pays debts? → The business — liability shielded (eliminates partnership)
  3. How funded? → No shareholders, no board (eliminates corporation)
Key PointLLC = shielded liability + no shareholders/board. The middle structure.

Practice — 6 questions

Read → pause → commit to an answer → then reveal.
4 on one stimulus (Tumble Route)· 2 standalone
Stimulus

Scenario

Aiko Mori and Jordan Lee started Tumble Route, a laundry pickup-and-delivery service, with a spoken agreement and no legal filings. Aiko manages the routes, the machines, and the schedule. Jordan handles pricing, advertising, and the accounts.

Last month a customer's designer jacket was ruined in a machine. The customer demands $800. The partners are weighing two options:

1. Reorganizing as an LLC 2. Incorporating so they can sell shares to finance 20 new vans

> Watch for the artifacts: a spoken partnership, a liability event, and two structure-change options.

Practice Question
Q1Liability for the ruined jacket

Aiko and Jordan formed Tumble Route with a spoken agreement and no legal filings. A customer's jacket was ruined. Which best describes who is liable for the $800 claim?

AOnly Aiko, because she manages the machines
BOnly Jordan, because he handles the accounts
CThe business alone — neither Aiko nor Jordan is personally liable
DBoth Aiko and Jordan are personally liable

Pause — which structure is it, and what does that structure say about liability?

Answer D

Why

A spoken agreement with no legal filings = a partnership. In a partnership, all partners are personally liable for business debts — regardless of whose area the damage occurred in.

A — the exam's favorite partnership trap. Liability is shared, not divided by who manages the machines. Aiko runs the machines, but Jordan is still liable.
B — same trap in reverse. Jordan handles accounts, but the jacket wasn't damaged by accounting. It doesn't matter — both partners are liable.
C — borrows the LLC / corporation rule. No filings = no liability shield. The business is the partners.
Key PointPartnership liability is shared and personal — not divided by who did the work.
Practice Question
Q2Classify the role split

Aiko manages routes, machines, and the schedule. Jordan handles pricing, advertising, and accounts. This arrangement best illustrates which concept?

APartners dividing roles by strengths and interests
BDepartments forming within an organizational structure
COutsourcing specialized functions to external firms
DA corporation electing a board of directors
Answer A

Why

Two partners with a spoken agreement, each handling the areas that match their skills — logistics for Aiko, business operations for Jordan. This is partners dividing roles by strengths and interests.

B — departments form when a business grows large enough to need specialized teams with managers. Two partners splitting work is not departments — scale is the tell.
Key PointPartners dividing roles ≠ departments. The difference is scale and specialization.
Practice Question
Q3Tradeoff of incorporating

If Tumble Route incorporates and sells shares to finance 20 new vans, which best describes the tradeoff?

AThe business loses its liability shield but gains funding
BThe partners gain more personal control over daily decisions
CThe owners cede control to shareholders and a board in exchange for greater access to funding
DThe business can no longer grow because shareholders limit decision making
Answer C

Why

Incorporating means selling shares — shareholders elect a board, and the founders cede control over major decisions. In exchange, the corporation gains the greatest access to funding — exactly what 20 new vans require.

A — flips the liability direction. Incorporating keeps the liability shield (same as LLC). The trade is control for funding, not liability for funding.
D — reverses the relationship. Shareholders enable growth by providing funding. The limitation is on the founders' control, not on the business's ability to grow.
Key PointControl and funding pull in opposite directions. Incorporating shrinks personal liability, cedes control, and unlocks funding.

A student studies three businesses and records the following data:

Answer D

Run the three questions for each business:

Practice Question
Q6Outsourcing support due to high wages

A software company hires an outside firm to run its customer-support phone lines because support wages in its city are unusually high. Which best describes this decision?

AThe company has made customer support one of its core competencies
BThe company has converted the support staff into internal stakeholders
CThe company is outsourcing a function because doing so is likely to reduce costs
DThe company has reorganized as a corporation
Answer C

Why

Outsourcing = hiring another business to handle a function. The two classic triggers are missing skills and high labor costs — high support wages are the second trigger.

A — core competencies are capabilities a business develops itself. Handing a function to an outside firm is the opposite move — support is a cost to manage, so the function moves outside.
Key PointOutsourcing ≠ core competency. One moves a function out; the other develops one in-house.
