LO 2.6.A — How products reach customers

Part 1: Types of Marketing Channels

Lesson

What is "Place"?

Place
where and how customers access products.

It is one of the 4 Ps of marketing (Product, Price, Place, Promotion).

Examples of "Place":

  • A retail store shelf
  • A company website
  • A vending machine
  • A mobile app
  • A membership warehouse
Key Pointplace = the whole path factory → customer's hands, not one location
Lesson

ConceptWhat It Covers

Direct vs. Indirect channels

⬇️ Direct

Producer → Customer

Path: No intermediaries.

Example: Brand website, company-owned store.

↔️ Indirect

Producer → Intermediary → Customer

Path: Through wholesalers, retailers, or distributors.

Example: Target, Amazon, grocery stores.

Intermediaries
any third party between the producer and the customer.
IntermediaryWhat They Do
WholesalerBuys in bulk from manufacturer, sells in smaller quantities to retailers
RetailerSells directly to the final customer (stores, websites)
DistributorBuys finished products from manufacturer, sells onward to retailers
Key PointDirect = no middleman. Indirect = one or more middlemen.

LO 2.6.B — Choosing where to sell

Part 2: Selecting Marketing Channels

Lesson

The channel selection decision

Choosing a channel is a strategic trade-off:

FactorDirect ChannelIndirect Channel
ControlHigh — you set price, brand, experienceLow — partners influence how it's sold
Cost to buildHigh — stores, websites, logistics, expertiseLow — partners already have infrastructure
ReachLimited — your own footprint onlyBroader — partners' customer base and locations
Expertise neededMust build sales and distribution skillsPartners bring existing expertise
Customer experienceConsistent, branded end-to-endVaries by partner quality
Profit per unitHigher — no intermediary cutLower — intermediaries take a share
Key Pointno “right” channel — depends on product · market · resources
Lesson

When to choose a direct channel

Choose direct when:

  • You want full control over pricing, branding, and customer experience
  • Your product is premium or differentiated — the experience matters
  • You have the resources to build stores, websites, and logistics
  • You want higher profit margins per sale

Examples:

  • Apple Stores — full control over the experience
  • Tesla — sells directly, no dealerships
  • Dyson — company-owned demo stores
Key Pointdirect: build more, but more control + profit per sale
Lesson

When to choose an indirect channel

Choose indirect when:

  • You need fast, wide market access — customers are everywhere
  • You lack the resources to build your own distribution
  • Your product is mass-market — convenience matters more than experience
  • Partners have expertise and networks you don't have

Examples:

  • A new snack brand selling through grocery store chains
  • A cosmetics brand selling through Sephora
  • An electronics brand selling through Best Buy
Key Pointindirect: reach + expertise — but less control + margin

Stanley — When channels go too far

By 2024, Stanley faced market saturation:

  • Too many colors, too many drops — customers felt overwhelmed
  • Resellers bought limited editions and sold them at 3–5x markup — frustrating real fans
  • Some customers began hoarding Stanley cups they never used
  • Critics said Stanley was creating artificial scarcity
Exam Notetoo much channel → overexposure + fake scarcity erode trust

观夏 — The WeChat mini-program as a channel

观夏 sells through its WeChat mini-program — but not like a normal online store:

  • Products are only available during scheduled "drops" (e.g., every Thursday at a specific time)
  • Most products sell out within minutes
  • Customers must follow 观夏's WeChat account to know when drops happen
  • No search, no browse, no "add to cart anytime" — scarcity by design

Why this works as a channel:

  • Creates urgency and exclusivity — owning 观夏 feels special
  • Drives social media buzz — customers share screenshots of successful purchases
  • Collects customer data directly — 观夏 knows exactly who buys and when
  • Maintains full pricing control — no discounts, no platform promotions
Exam Notemini-program = a channel that makes purchase an event (scheduled drops)

Scenario

Brightfield Solar makes portable solar panels for camping and outdoor use.

YearSales ChannelRevenueProfit Margin
1Company website (DTC)$200,00035%
2Company website + REI (outdoor retailer)$800,00025%
3Website + REI + Amazon$2,000,00018%

In Year 5, Brightfield is deciding between: - Option A: Continue adding retailers (Costco, Target) - Option B: Invest in the DTC website to increase direct sales

Practice Question
Q1Q1
In Year 2, Brightfield added REI as a retail partner and revenue quadrupled. Why did adding an indirect channel increase revenue so dramatically?
AREI lowered the price, making the product more affordable
BREI gave Brightfield access to REI's existing customer base and physical store locations
CREI took over manufacturing, reducing Brightfield's costs
DREI replaced Brightfield's website, simplifying operations
Answer B

Answer: B.

REI is an indirect channel (retailer) that already has millions of outdoor enthusiasts shopping in its stores and on its website. By partnering with REI, Brightfield gained access to REI's customer base and physical locations — reach that the DTC website alone could not provide.

A — There is no evidence that REI lowered the price; in fact, adding a retailer typically increases the retail price (retailer takes a margin)
C — Retailers do not manufacture products; REI sells, not makes
D — Brightfield still had its website in Year 2; REI was added alongside it, not instead of it
Key PointIndirect channels increase revenue by giving you access to the partner's existing customers and locations.
Practice Question
Q2Q2
From Year 1 to Year 4, Brightfield's profit margin dropped from 35% to 12%. What is the most likely explanation?
ABrightfield's manufacturing costs increased each year
BEach new channel partner takes a share of revenue, reducing Brightfield's per-unit profit
CCustomers prefer buying from retailers, so they stopped buying from the website
DThe product quality declined as sales volume increased
Answer B

Answer: B.

Each indirect channel (REI, Amazon, Walmart) takes a percentage of revenue as their margin. The more intermediaries in the channel, the less profit Brightfield keeps per sale. DTC (website) has no intermediary cut — 35% margin. Adding REI drops it. Adding Amazon drops it more. Adding Walmart drops it further.

A — Manufacturing costs are not mentioned; the question links margin decline to channel additions
C — The website still exists and generates revenue; the issue is margin, not customer preference
D — No evidence of quality decline; the issue is structural (intermediary margins), not product-related
> More intermediaries = more reach = less profit per unit. That is the trade-off.
Practice Question
Q3Q3
If Brightfield chooses Option B (invest in DTC), which channel advantage are they prioritizing?
AReach — getting the product in front of more customers
BControl — maintaining pricing power and the direct customer relationship
CCost reduction — eliminating manufacturing expenses
DLegal compliance — avoiding regulatory requirements
Answer B

Answer: B.

DTC (selling through your own website) gives Brightfield full control over pricing, branding, customer experience, and customer data. By investing in DTC, Brightfield is prioritizing control and margin over the reach that retail partners provide.

A — DTC has LESS reach than adding retailers; Brightfield is trading reach for control
C — DTC does not reduce manufacturing costs; it reduces intermediary costs (retailer margins)
D — Solar panels do not have legally mandated channels; this is irrelevant
Key PointDTC = more control and margin, but less reach. Retailers = more reach, but less control and margin.
Practice Question
Q4Q4
A new competitor enters the market and sells their solar panels exclusively on Amazon. What type of channel is this?
ADirect + B2C
BIndirect + B2C
CDirect + B2B
DIndirect + B2B
Answer B

Answer: B.

Selling on Amazon means the competitor is using Amazon as an intermediary (indirect channel) to reach individual consumers (B2C). Amazon is a retailer that sits between the producer and the customer.

A — Amazon is a third-party platform — it is an intermediary, not a direct channel
C and D — Amazon sells to consumers, not to other businesses (in this context)
Key PointSelling ON a platform (Amazon, Tmall, JD) = indirect channel. Selling FROM your own website = direct channel.
Practice Question
Q5Q5
Brightfield discovers that a prescription-strength sunscreen manufacturer is required by law to sell only through licensed pharmacies. This is an example of:
AA B2B channel
BA direct channel
CA legally mandated channel
DA competitive pricing strategy
Answer C

Answer: C.

The law requires the sunscreen to be sold only through licensed pharmacies — this is a legally mandated channel. The manufacturer cannot choose to sell through Amazon, Target, or its own website.

A — Pharmacies sell to consumers (B2C), not to other businesses
B — The pharmacy is an intermediary, making this an indirect channel — but the key point is that the law mandates this channel
D — This question is about Place (channels), not Price
Key PointLegally mandated channels override all other channel considerations — the law decides, not the business.
Practice Question
Q6Q6
Stanley sold Quencher tumblers through hardware stores before 2019, targeting construction workers. After 2019, Stanley sold through Target, targeting young women. What does this channel shift illustrate about the relationship between target customer and channel selection?
AHardware stores are always a poor channel for consumer products
BChannel selection should match the target customer — when the customer changes, the channel must change
CTarget is a better retailer than hardware stores for all products
DStanley should have kept both channels and not changed anything
Answer B

Answer: B.

Stanley's channel shift illustrates a core principle: the channel must match the customer. Hardware stores reached construction workers (the old customer) but could never reach young women (the new customer). Target's shoppers matched the new target demographic perfectly.

A — Hardware stores are excellent channels for tools and construction products; the issue is customer-channel fit, not store quality
C — Target is not universally "better"; it is better for THIS customer segment
D — Keeping both channels could work (and Stanley does maintain some old channels), but the PRIMARY channel had to shift to reach the new customer
Key PointWhen the target customer changes, the channel must change. The product stays the same — the PLACE must follow the customer.

Wrap-up

Wrap-up — 2.6 Place and Channels

What we covered:

- Place = where and how customers access products
- Marketing channel = the final stage of the supply chain
- Direct channels = no intermediaries (website, company store)
- Indirect channels = intermediaries between producer and customer (wholesaler, retailer)
- B2C = sell to consumers; B2B = sell to businesses
- Legally mandated channels = law decides where the product can be sold
  • Channel selection trade-off: control vs. reach, margin vs. convenience
  • Stanley: hardware → Target + DTC → new customer → $70M → $750M
