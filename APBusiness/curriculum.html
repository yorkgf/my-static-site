Course Overview and Skills

COURSE SKILLS

These skills spiral across units, revisited and built upon throughout the course. Students practice these skills through business cases, the Business Canvas Project, and the Financial Advisor Project.

  • 1 Concept Application
    Explain business and personal finance concepts, principles, and strategies, and apply them to scenarios.
  • 2 Entrepreneurship
    Identify a market opportunity, and develop and test a product idea designed to address the opportunity.
  • 3 Decision Making
    Describe opportunities or problems, and recommend courses of action to address them.
  • 4 Communication
    Create authentic communications appropriate for a specific audience and purpose.
  • 5 Collaboration
    Work collaboratively with and lead others to accomplish a goal or task.

BIG IDEAS (SPIRALED ACROSS UNITS)

Core concepts that connect content across all units:

  • 1 Value Creation — How businesses create value for customers through products that address problems, needs, and wants
  • 2 Competitive Advantage — Differentiation and market positioning strategies to outperform rivals
  • 3 Financial Health — Profitability, cash flow, and sustainability for both businesses and individuals
  • 4 Ethical Leadership — Responsible business practices considering stakeholders and societal impact

Note: Units 1-4 are assessed on the AP Exam (May 3-14). Unit 5 (Personal Goals, Budgeting, and Investing) is NOT assessed on the AP Exam and can be taught before or after.

Unit 1 - Businesses, Competition, and New Ideas (20-30%)

Learning Objectives

  • Identify ways businesses address customers' problems, needs, and wants through value creation.
  • Distinguish between value creation and value capture in business models.
  • Explain how sellers and buyers interact in a market, establishing a market price.
  • Develop or evaluate a business's plan to achieve competitive advantage in a market.
  • Describe the PESTEL factors (Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental, Legal) that shape markets.
  • Apply the PESTEL framework to evaluate the attractiveness and potential risks of a market.
  • Describe strategies entrepreneurs and businesses use to generate new product ideas.
  • Apply an entrepreneurial design-thinking process to generate and validate new business ideas.
  • Explain how core values and core competencies shape business and individual decision making.
  • Develop or evaluate a vision statement and mission statement based on business goals and values.
  • Explain how and why businesses encourage ethical behavior and respond to ethical dilemmas.
  • Describe major types of business organization (sole proprietorship, partnership, LLC, corporation) and their advantages/disadvantages.
  • Describe factors businesses consider when developing production processes and supply chains.

Curriculum Standards

  • The unit will take approximately 37 class periods to complete (Weeks 1-7, plus ~12-14 days for Business Canvas Project).
  • This unit contributes to a 20% to 30% weight in the AP exam.
  • College Board CED Topics covered: 1.1–1.8
  • Course Skills: All five skills (Concept Application, Entrepreneurship, Decision Making, Communication, Collaboration) are introduced and practiced in this unit.

Guiding Questions

  • What is a business, and how does it create value for customers?
  • How do businesses achieve competitive advantage in competitive markets?
  • What are PESTEL factors, and how do they influence business viability?
  • How do entrepreneurs identify and validate market opportunities?
  • What role do vision and mission statements play in guiding business decisions?
  • How should business leaders respond to ethical dilemmas?
  • What are the advantages and disadvantages of different business organizational structures?
  • How do supply chain decisions impact competitive advantage?

Key Concepts

  • Value Creation vs. Value Capture: Creating customer value vs. charging more than production cost.
  • Competitive Advantage: Outperforming rivals through differentiation, low cost, or barriers to entry.
  • PESTEL Framework: Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental, Legal factors shaping markets.
  • Design Thinking Process: Observe → Validate → Ideate → Prototype → Test (MVP).
  • Vision & Mission: Vision describes aspirations; mission describes how to achieve long-term goals.
  • Business Ethics: Codes of conduct, stakeholder impact analysis, ethical decision-making frameworks.
  • Business Organizations: Sole proprietorship, partnership, LLC, corporation (control vs. liability trade-offs).
  • Supply Chains: From raw materials to final customer delivery; cost, quality, efficiency, risk considerations.

Key Vocabulary

  • Business: An organization that produces and distributes products (goods and/or services) to address customer problems, needs, and wants.
  • Market Opportunity: A customer problem, need, or want that a business can address profitably.
  • Competitive Advantage: The ability to outperform rivals in the same market, leading to increased market share and profits.
  • PESTEL Factors: External forces (Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental, Legal) that impact business viability.
  • Minimum Viable Product (MVP): The simplest version of a product idea with only core features, used to validate with customers.
  • Core Values: Defining beliefs and principles that guide an individual's or business's actions.
  • Ethical Dilemma: When a core value conflicts with other values or with business goals or practices.
  • Sole Proprietorship: A business owned by one person who retains control and profits but has unlimited personal liability.
  • Supply Chain: All individuals and businesses involved in producing and distributing a product, from raw materials to final delivery.

Unit 2 - Marketing (20-30%)

Learning Objectives

  • Explain why and how marketers collect customer data (demographic and psychographic).
  • Use market segmentation to identify target customers and develop customer profiles.
  • Describe the purpose of building customer relationships and develop a relationship plan.
  • Explain how collection and use of customer data creates privacy risks for individuals and businesses.
  • Explain why and how consumers make buying decisions (rational process, personal, psychological, social factors).
  • Evaluate sales tactics using Cialdini's principles of influence (scarcity, authority, consensus, liking, reciprocity, consistency, unity).
  • Explain why businesses conduct market research (desirability, feasibility, viability).
  • Conduct and interpret primary-source and secondary-source research to assess market opportunities.
  • Develop and interpret data visualizations (bar charts, line graphs, pie charts) for market research.
  • Describe the six stages of product development and develop an MVP and value proposition.
  • Develop and evaluate branding for a business or product.
  • Explain how businesses adapt marketing strategies throughout the product life cycle.
  • Develop and evaluate pricing strategies (value-based, competitive, cost-based, penetration).
  • Select and evaluate marketing channels (B2C, B2B, direct, indirect) for a product.
  • Develop or evaluate a marketing campaign using the promotional mix.

Curriculum Standards

  • The unit will take approximately 34 class periods to complete (Weeks 8-12, plus ~13-15 days for Business Canvas Project).
  • This unit contributes to a 20% to 30% weight in the AP exam.
  • College Board CED Topics covered: 2.1–2.7
  • Course Skills: Marketing applications emphasize Communication (4.A, 4.B) and Concept Application (1.A-1.C).

Guiding Questions

  • How do businesses identify and profile target customers?
  • What factors influence consumer buying decisions?
  • How do businesses use market research to reduce risk and inform decisions?
  • What is the promotional mix, and when should each tool be used?
  • How do pricing strategies impact profitability and market share?
  • What are the advantages and disadvantages of different marketing channels?
  • How does the product life cycle influence marketing strategy?

Key Concepts

  • Market Segmentation: Grouping customers by shared demographic/psychographic characteristics.
  • Cialdini's Principles: Scarcity, authority, consensus, liking, reciprocity, consistency, unity.
  • Primary vs. Secondary Research: Original data collection vs. existing sources.
  • Product Development Stages: Ideation → Validation → Design → Messaging → Production → Launch.
  • Value Proposition: Who, what problem, how superior to alternatives.
  • Product Life Cycle: Introduction → Growth → Maturity → Decline.
  • Pricing Strategies: Value-based, competitive, cost-based, penetration pricing.
  • Promotional Mix: Media advertising, personal selling, sales promotion, direct marketing, public relations.

Key Vocabulary

  • Target Customer: Buyers most likely to purchase a product based on wants, needs, and preferences.
  • Customer Profile: Fictional description of a sample customer incorporating demographic and psychographic data.
  • Customer Acquisition Cost: Total marketing/sales costs divided by number of new customers acquired.
  • Lifetime Value: Estimated total revenue from a customer over time.
  • Minimum Viable Product (MVP): Simplest product version with core features for customer validation.
  • Branding: Developing an identity (name, symbol, design) to distinguish from competitors.
  • Price Elasticity: Measure of customer responsiveness to price changes (excluded from AP Exam).
  • Marketing Channel: The individuals/businesses required to deliver a finished product to the final customer.
  • Digital Marketing: Using internet/digital tools (websites, email, social media) to connect with customers.

Unit 3 - Personal Saving, Borrowing, and Business Finance (25-35%)

Learning Objectives - Part 1: Personal Saving & Borrowing

  • Describe reasons consumers save and identify barriers to saving (inconsistent income, impulse buying).
  • Explain how PESTEL factors impact the value of savings and incentives to save.
  • Develop or evaluate a savings plan (savings accounts, money market, CDs).
  • Describe reasons consumers borrow and types of credit (credit cards, loans, mortgages).
  • Explain how interest rates, terms, and credit scores affect borrowing costs.
  • Evaluate strategies to manage debt and maintain good credit health.
  • Describe potential consequences of excessive debt and bankruptcy.

Learning Objectives - Part 2: Business Finance & Accounting

  • Explain the purpose of accounting and financial management for business health.
  • Distinguish between revenue, expenses, profit, and cash flow.
  • Interpret and analyze the three main financial statements: Income Statement, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow Statement.
  • Calculate and interpret key financial ratios (profitability, liquidity, efficiency).
  • Explain how businesses secure financial capital (equity vs. debt financing).
  • Evaluate ethical considerations in financial reporting and accounting practices.

Curriculum Standards

  • Part 1 (Personal): ~6 class periods (can be integrated into other units or taught separately).
  • Part 2 (Business): ~29 class periods (Weeks 14-17).
  • Total Unit 3: ~35 class periods
  • This unit contributes to a 25% to 35% weight in the AP exam.
  • College Board CED Topics covered: 3.1–3.9
  • Course Skills: Decision Making (3.A-3.D) and Financial Literacy applications are emphasized.

Guiding Questions

  • Why do individuals save, and what barriers prevent effective saving?
  • How do interest rates and credit scores impact borrowing costs?
  • What are the three main financial statements, and what does each reveal about a business?
  • How do businesses balance profitability with cash flow management?
  • What are the advantages and disadvantages of debt vs. equity financing?
  • Why is ethical financial reporting critical for business sustainability?

Key Concepts

  • Time Value of Money: Money today is worth more than money in the future (interest).
  • Compound Interest: Interest earned on both principal and accumulated interest.
  • Credit Score: Numerical representation of creditworthiness (300-850).
  • Income Statement: Revenue - Expenses = Profit (period of time).
  • Balance Sheet: Assets = Liabilities + Equity (point in time).
  • Cash Flow Statement: Operating, investing, financing activities (cash inflows/outflows).
  • Financial Ratios: Profitability (ROI, profit margin), Liquidity (current ratio), Efficiency (inventory turnover).
  • Break-Even Point: Where total revenue equals total costs (no profit, no loss).

Key Vocabulary

  • Asset: Something of value owned by an individual or business (cash, inventory, equipment).
  • Liability: A debt or obligation owed to others (loans, accounts payable).
  • Net Worth: Assets minus liabilities (personal or business equity).
  • Interest Rate: The cost of borrowing money or return on savings, expressed as a percentage.
  • APR (Annual Percentage Rate): Annual cost of borrowing including fees.
  • Revenue: Total income from sales of goods or services (before expenses).
  • Profit: Revenue remaining after all expenses are paid (net income).
  • Cash Flow: The movement of cash into and out of a business over time.
  • Depreciation: The decrease in value of an asset over time due to wear and tear or obsolescence.

Unit 4 - Management and Strategy (15-20%)

Learning Objectives

  • Describe management and leadership roles, styles, and responsibilities.
  • Explain how organizational culture and structure impact business performance.
  • Evaluate business performance using Key Performance Indicators (KPIs).
  • Develop or evaluate strategies using Porter's Five Forces framework.
  • Develop or evaluate strategies using SWOT analysis (Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats).
  • Explain how businesses use strategic decision-making to achieve competitive advantage.
  • Describe how businesses manage growth, innovation, and change.
  • Evaluate the role of human resources in talent acquisition, development, and retention.

Curriculum Standards

  • The unit will take approximately 30 class periods to complete (Weeks 19-23).
  • This unit contributes to a 15% to 20% weight in the AP exam.
  • College Board CED Topics covered: 4.1–4.4
  • Course Skills: Decision Making (3.A-3.D) and Strategic Thinking are emphasized in this unit.

Guiding Questions

  • What are the differences between management and leadership?
  • How do businesses measure and evaluate performance using KPIs?
  • How do Porter's Five Forces shape industry competitiveness?
  • When should businesses use SWOT analysis for strategic planning?
  • How do businesses manage organizational change and innovation?
  • What role does organizational culture play in business success?

Key Concepts

  • Management vs. Leadership: Management focuses on efficiency and control; leadership focuses on vision and inspiration.
  • Porter's Five Forces: Threat of new entrants, supplier power, buyer power, substitute products, competitive rivalry.
  • SWOT Analysis: Internal strengths/weaknesses, external opportunities/threats.
  • KPIs: Quantifiable metrics to evaluate business performance (revenue growth, profit margin, customer satisfaction).
  • Organizational Structure: Hierarchical, flat, matrix, divisional structures.
  • Change Management: Strategies for managing organizational transitions and innovation.

Key Vocabulary

  • Leadership Style: Autocratic, democratic, laissez-faire, transformational, servant leadership.
  • Organizational Culture: Shared values, beliefs, and norms that influence how employees think and behave.
  • KPI (Key Performance Indicator): A measurable value that demonstrates how effectively a company is achieving key objectives.
  • Porter's Five Forces: Framework for analyzing industry competitiveness and profitability.
  • SWOT Analysis: Strategic planning tool to identify Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats.
  • Strategic Decision: High-stakes decision that affects long-term direction and competitive position.
  • Talent Management: HR processes for attracting, developing, and retaining employees.

Unit 5 - Personal Goals, Budgeting, and Investing (NOT on AP Exam)

Learning Objectives

  • Explain how taxes impact net income and personal budgeting decisions.
  • Develop a personal budget based on income, expenses, and financial goals.
  • Describe strategies for managing personal risk (insurance, emergency funds).
  • Explain the importance of insurance (health, auto, home, life, disability).
  • Describe investment vehicles (stocks, bonds, mutual funds, ETFs, real estate).
  • Explain the relationship between risk and return in investment decisions.
  • Develop a personal investment strategy for education, housing, and retirement goals.
  • Explain the power of compound interest and time horizon in wealth building.
  • Evaluate the impact of inflation on long-term financial planning.

Curriculum Standards

  • Note: This unit is NOT assessed on the AP Exam.
  • Can be taught before or after the AP Exam based on school calendar.
  • The unit will take approximately 24 class periods to complete.
  • An additional 20 class periods are recommended for expanded personal finance coverage.
  • College Board CED Topics covered: 5.1–5.3
  • Financial Advisor Project: Culminating project where students provide financial recommendations to a fictional household.

Guiding Questions

  • How do taxes impact take-home pay and budgeting decisions?
  • What are the key components of a personal budget, and how do you create one?
  • What types of insurance are necessary for financial protection?
  • What is the relationship between risk and return in investing?
  • How does compound interest work, and why does time horizon matter?
  • How do you plan for long-term goals like education, homeownership, and retirement?

Key Concepts

  • Net Income: Gross income minus taxes and deductions.
  • 50/30/20 Rule: 50% needs, 30% wants, 20% savings/debt repayment.
  • Emergency Fund: 3-6 months of living expenses saved for unexpected events.
  • Risk-Return Tradeoff: Higher potential returns come with higher risk.
  • Diversification: Spreading investments across asset classes to reduce risk.
  • Compound Interest: Earning returns on both principal and accumulated returns.
  • Time Horizon: Longer time horizons can tolerate more risk and benefit more from compounding.

Key Vocabulary

  • Gross Income: Total income before taxes and deductions.
  • Net Income: Take-home pay after taxes and deductions.
  • Stock: Ownership share in a corporation (equity investment).
  • Bond: Debt investment where you lend money to an entity for a fixed return.
  • Mutual Fund: Pooled investment vehicle managed by a professional.
  • ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund): A basket of securities traded on an exchange like a stock.
  • 401(k)/403(b): Employer-sponsored retirement savings plans with tax advantages.
  • Roth IRA: Retirement account funded with after-tax dollars; withdrawals are tax-free.
  • Inflation: The rate at which prices for goods and services rise, eroding purchasing power.

Course Projects

Business Canvas Project (Units 1-4)

Students develop a complete business model throughout Units 1-4, applying concepts from each unit to their own entrepreneurial idea.

Project Tasks:

  • Task 1: Identify and describe a customer's problem, need, or want
  • Task 2: Develop a plan to seek competitive advantage; apply PESTEL framework
  • Task 3: Conduct customer interviews to validate the problem
  • Task 4: Develop a solution (product idea)
  • Task 5: Formulate and test business hypotheses
  • Task 6: Develop vision and mission statements
  • Task 7: Develop production process and supply chain plan
  • Task 8: Commit to business idea and revise market analysis
  • Marketing Tasks: Target customer profile, customer relationships, sales tactics, market research, MVP/branding, pricing, channels, marketing campaign
  • Finance Tasks: Financial projections (revenue, expenses, profit, cash flow)

AP Exam Day Validation:

Students will complete a free-response question on the AP Exam requiring them to explain how they used hypothesis testing to inform decision-making during their Business Canvas Project.

Financial Advisor Project (Unit 5)

Students apply personal finance concepts to provide financial recommendations to a fictional household based on a provided financial profile.

Project Components:

  • Household Profile Analysis: Review financial situation (income, expenses, assets, liabilities, goals)
  • Budget Recommendation: Develop monthly budget aligned with financial goals
  • Emergency Fund Plan: Calculate 3-6 months of expenses
